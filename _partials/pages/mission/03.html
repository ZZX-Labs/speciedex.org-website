The Problem

Biodiversity knowledge is vast, essential, unequally accessible, and fragmented across scientific, institutional, technical, and legal boundaries.

Fragmentation also obscures disagreement. Two authoritative providers may represent the same organism differently, use different accepted names, or disagree about rank and lineage. A responsible infrastructure system must preserve those differences and their provenance rather than silently flattening them.

Scientific names change. Classifications are revised. Datasets disappear. Web services close. Publications become inaccessible. Media loses attribution. Geographic records become detached from their original sources. Provider identifiers are replaced. The result is a global body of knowledge that is valuable but difficult to preserve, reconcile, and reuse.

These resources often use different identifiers, taxonomic systems, schemas, interfaces, licensing terms, geographic conventions, update schedules, and technical standards. Important information may be duplicated, isolated, difficult to compare, inaccessible to the public, or dependent on aging software and institutions.

Scientific knowledge about species is distributed across universities, museums, journals, field stations, government agencies, conservation organizations, independent databases, specimen collections, genetic repositories, archives, private research systems, and historical publications.

Fragmented Information

Species records remain distributed across incompatible databases, publications, archives, collections, and institutional systems.

Unstable References

Links, identifiers, providers, interfaces, and software may disappear or change without preserving historical context.

Unequal Access

Researchers, educators, communities, and the public do not have equal access to scientific literature, tools, and biodiversity data.

Loss of Attribution

Authors, collectors, institutions, licenses, and revision histories are frequently separated from reused information.

Preservation Risk

Valuable records may depend on a single institution, server, funding program, account, vendor, or proprietary format.

Limited Interoperability

Inconsistent schemas and identifiers make comparison, reconciliation, validation, and exchange difficult across systems.

Hidden Uncertainty

Conflicting classifications, provisional names, disputed records, and incomplete evidence are often collapsed into false certainty.

Sensitive-Data Risk

Open publication can expose endangered species, protected habitats, private contributors, collections, or culturally governed knowledge.
