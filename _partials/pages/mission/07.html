Who the Mission Serves

The mission serves multiple audiences through different interfaces, but all should be able to trace public information back to the same underlying provenance and validation state.

Researchers

Access structured records, sources, identifiers, datasets, revision histories, exports, maps, literature, genetics, and analytical tools.

Taxonomists

Review names, authorities, synonyms, classifications, specimens, publications, provider differences, and competing interpretations.

Conservationists

Connect distribution, habitat, ecological, threat, conservation-status, and supporting research records while protecting sensitive locations.

Museums and Collections

Link specimens, catalogues, collections, publications, media, and institutional identifiers to stable taxonomic references.

Institutions

Exchange and preserve data through open standards while retaining attribution, provenance, licensing, and institutional identity.

Educators and Students

Explore biodiversity through accessible descriptions, media, maps, references, classifications, timelines, and educational resources.

Developers

Build tools, visualizations, applications, archives, integrations, and research systems using public APIs, schemas, datasets, and libraries.

Governments and NGOs

Use transparent biodiversity information for research, conservation planning, education, policy, and public services.

Archivists and Librarians

Preserve releases, provider snapshots, literature, documentation, metadata, and historical states in durable formats.

The Public

Discover life on Earth and participate in the study, preservation, and responsible stewardship of biodiversity knowledge.
