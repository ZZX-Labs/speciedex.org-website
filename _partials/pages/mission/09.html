What Success Looks Like

The project should be evaluated through a balanced set of measures: scientific coverage, data quality, preserved provenance, public usability, interoperability, resilience, participation, reproducibility, and the ability to survive beyond a single institution or maintainer.

Success is not defined only by the number of records stored in the system. A large database without provenance, accessibility, verification, resilience, scientific context, or ethical publication would not fulfill the mission.

Coverage

Broad representation of known life across kingdoms, regions, habitats, taxonomic ranks, providers, and historical periods.

Quality

Records supported by traceable sources, explicit validation states, review, and documented uncertainty.

Provenance

Source providers, original identifiers, licenses, retrieval dates, transformations, and revision history remain connected to canonical records.

Accessibility

Public browsing, exports, APIs, multilingual interfaces, educational tools, and low-bandwidth or offline access.

Interoperability

Stable identifiers, documented schemas, compatible formats, and meaningful links to external scientific resources.

Resilience

Independent mirrors, archives, reproducible deployments, portable datasets, and recovery procedures.

Reproducibility

Published inputs, transformations, schemas, code, manifests, and outputs can be independently reconstructed.

Participation

Meaningful contributions from researchers, institutions, developers, educators, communities, archivists, and volunteers.
