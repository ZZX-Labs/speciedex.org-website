Our Commitments

Commitments define the standards by which the project’s behavior should be evaluated, even when those standards make development slower or more demanding.

Open Software

Core software should remain inspectable, auditable, reproducible, and available under permissive open-source licensing where project-controlled.

Open Standards

Public schemas, stable identifiers, documented interfaces, and portable formats should support interoperability and independent implementation.

Scientific Transparency

Records should distinguish accepted information, provisional claims, disputed classifications, historical names, inference, and unresolved uncertainty.

Source Attribution

Authors, researchers, providers, institutions, collectors, publishers, creators, licenses, and source records should remain connected to reused information.

Public Accountability

Material technical, scientific, archival, and governance decisions should be documented and reviewable whenever practical.

Responsible Publication

Open access must be balanced against conservation risk, privacy, legal restrictions, research ethics, cultural responsibility, and data sovereignty.

Long-Term Availability

Datasets, releases, documentation, schemas, signatures, manifests, and historical records should be preserved beyond individual services and release cycles.

Independent Operation

Qualified organizations and individuals should be able to operate mirrors, archives, software deployments, and verification systems independently.

Reversible Transformation

Normalization and reconciliation should retain enough source information to explain or reverse transformations where technically possible.

Continuity Planning

Documentation, archives, release procedures, credentials, and operating knowledge should survive changes in maintainers or institutions.
