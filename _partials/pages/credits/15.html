Decentralized Infrastructure

Bitcoin

Bitcoin, its contributors, and the cypherpunk tradition provide an important technical and philosophical reference for open verification, distributed operation, public infrastructure, and financial autonomy.

We thank Satoshi Nakamoto for publishing the Bitcoin white paper, releasing the original software, mining the genesis block, and giving the world a working example of decentralized, censorship-resistant, peer-to-peer money.

We thank Bitcoin, Bitcoin Core, every Bitcoin Core maintainer and contributor, every node operator, miner, researcher, educator, wallet developer, protocol engineer, archivist, translator, reviewer, tester, independent developer, and user who helped keep the network alive.

We thank Hal Finney, Len Sassaman, Dorian Nakamoto, Adam Back, Nick Szabo, Gavin Andresen, Martti Malmi, and the many early contributors, cryptographers, cypherpunks, advocates, testers, and builders whose lives, reputations, time, labor, courage, and sacrifice became part of Bitcoin’s history.

We remember Hal Finney for his intelligence, generosity, optimism, technical work, and participation in Bitcoin’s earliest days.

We remember Len Sassaman for his work in cryptography, privacy, remailers, anonymity, and the cypherpunk tradition.

We recognize Dorian Nakamoto and the profound personal intrusion he experienced after being publicly and incorrectly identified as Bitcoin’s creator.

We thank Adam Back for Hashcash, cryptographic research, cypherpunk work, and long-standing contributions to Bitcoin.

We thank Nick Szabo for foundational thinking about digital contracts, Bit Gold, trusted third parties, and decentralized digital value.

We thank Gavin Andresen for his role in Bitcoin’s early software development, testing, communication, and transition from its original creator.

We thank Martti Malmi for early Bitcoin development, documentation, website work, community support, and helping introduce Bitcoin to the world.

We thank the cypherpunks, cryptographers, computer scientists, economists, libertarians, activists, dissidents, journalists, whistleblowers, human-rights advocates, and ordinary people who preserved the idea that privacy, voluntary exchange, and financial autonomy matter.

Above all, we thank Satoshi Nakamoto for giving us hope.
