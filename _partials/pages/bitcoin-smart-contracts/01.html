Bitcoin Smart Contracts

A technical introduction to Bitcoin Script, multisignature policies, timelocks, hashlocks, payment channels, Taproot, Miniscript, output descriptors, Discreet Log Contracts, vaults, inheritance, and other programmable Bitcoin systems.

The Bitcoin Smart Contracts page explains how Bitcoin can enforce programmable spending conditions without turning every validating node into a general-purpose application server. It focuses on narrowly defined cryptographic policies that determine when, how, and by whom a transaction output may be spent.

Bitcoin smart contracts are built from signatures, thresholds, timelocks, hashlocks, transaction relationships, commitments, recovery paths, and carefully constrained scripts. These mechanisms support shared custody, inheritance, escrow, Lightning channels, atomic swaps, vaults, Discreet Log Contracts, milestone payments, and other coordinated financial arrangements.

Programmability does not eliminate risk. A contract can enforce the wrong policy perfectly, permanently lock funds, expose sensitive metadata, depend on unavailable keys or oracles, or become too expensive to execute under changing fee conditions. Every spending and recovery path must therefore be understood before funds are committed.

This page is educational. It explains contract mechanisms, limitations, design principles, development tools, and potential Speciedex applications without offering financial, legal, custody, estate-planning, security, or software-assurance advice.

Define Authorization

Require one signature, several signatures, a threshold of keys, or one of multiple permitted authorization paths.

Define Time

Delay spending, create refund paths, stage recovery, or coordinate channel and inheritance conditions.

Define Knowledge

Require disclosure of a cryptographic secret or commitment before a conditional payment can complete.

Define Recovery

Preserve alternate paths for emergencies, missing participants, failed cooperation, or long-term succession.
