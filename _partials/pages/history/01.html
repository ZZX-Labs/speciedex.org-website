The History of Speciedex

A chronological account of how Speciedex developed from an early theoretical concept around 2013 into an active biodiversity, software, archival, and public-infrastructure project in 2026.

The History page records how the project changed over time. It does not repeat the global header, the project overview, or the mission statement. Its purpose is to preserve the sequence of ideas, technical decisions, discarded approaches, renewed work, architectural divisions, and public-development milestones that shaped Speciedex.

Speciedex did not begin as a finished application. It began as a theoretical attempt to imagine a durable system capable of connecting species, classifications, research, observations, geography, media, evidence, and historical records within one extensible framework.

The project’s development has not followed a perfectly linear path. Requirements changed, technologies matured, earlier ideas were reconsidered, and the scope expanded from a species index into a coordinated ecosystem of public interfaces, data systems, archives, analysis tools, distributed services, and machine-readable infrastructure.

This history remains provisional where original notes, prototypes, source records, releases, and archived materials have not yet been formally cataloged. The timeline below preserves the current high-level account while leaving room for later archival correction and expansion.
