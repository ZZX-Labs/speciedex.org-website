Project Principles

The scientific, technical, archival, and public-interest standards guiding Speciedex design, operation, governance, and long-term development.

These principles are operational requirements rather than promotional language. They shape how records are imported, transformed, cited, corrected, distributed, archived, and presented; how software releases are verified; and how institutions and independent users can reproduce the project without dependence on a closed interface or single controlling service.

Open Access

Public biodiversity knowledge should remain accessible in documented, reusable formats without unnecessary restrictions, proprietary lock-in, or dependence on a single institution or interface.

Scientific Integrity

Records should be supported by traceable sources, taxonomic authorities, published research, reproducible methods, explicit uncertainty, and transparent revision histories.

Provider Provenance

Imported values should retain their source provider, original identifier, retrieval date, license, transformations, verification state, and relationship to the canonical record.

Long-Term Preservation

Species data, software, documentation, media, schemas, and research should remain available across generations, institutions, platforms, and changing technologies.

Independent Verification

Releases, datasets, records, and metadata should be verifiable through published checksums, signatures, manifests, source references, schemas, tests, and reproducible builds.

Interoperability

Open formats, stable identifiers, documented APIs, common schemas, and export tools should enable cooperation among researchers, institutions, software, and public datasets.

Distributed Resilience

Mirrors, independent operators, local archives, and redundant infrastructure should reduce reliance on any single server, organization, jurisdiction, or service provider.

Ethical and Legal Responsibility

Sensitive biological information, genetic references, specimen records, licensing, attribution, conservation risk, and legal restrictions must be handled transparently and carefully.
