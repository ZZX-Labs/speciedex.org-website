Lightning Network

A technical introduction to Bitcoin payment channels, routed payments, invoices, offers, liquidity, HTLCs, onion routing, Lightning nodes, LND, Core Lightning, Eclair, LDK, recovery, privacy, and secure application development.

The Lightning Network page explains how rapid Bitcoin payments can move through a decentralized channel network without recording every individual transfer directly in the Bitcoin blockchain. It focuses on the contracts, routing systems, liquidity, node software, custody models, recovery procedures, and operational responsibilities that make Lightning possible.

Lightning extends Bitcoin rather than replacing it. Participants commit bitcoin to on-chain contracts, exchange updated balances off-chain, route conditional payments through connected nodes, and retain the ability to settle channel state through the Bitcoin base layer.

Lightning can support retail payments, donations, micropayments, streaming value, international transfers, software services, contributor payments, research bounties, and machine-readable payment systems. These benefits depend on usable liquidity, maintained software, protected credentials, verified backups, and reliable Bitcoin infrastructure.

Lightning introduces operational complexity. Channel state, liquidity direction, force closures, on-chain fees, privacy leakage, custodial dependence, backup behavior, administrative credentials, and software defects can all affect the security or availability of funds.
