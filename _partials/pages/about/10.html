Governance

Transparent development, documented decisions, and independent review.

Governance must distinguish scientific authority from software maintenance, operational control, funding, and public communication. Decisions should be made by the people and institutions qualified for the relevant domain and recorded in a form that can be reviewed later.

Participation should remain possible for independent developers, researchers, institutions, contributors, archivists, and infrastructure operators. No single participant should be indispensable to the continued operation, verification, restoration, or preservation of the public system.

Speciedex is developed as an open-source project. Technical decisions, schema revisions, release planning, scientific standards, archival policies, and material governance choices should be documented publicly whenever practical.

Public Development

Source code, issue tracking, technical proposals, release notes, and contribution procedures should remain publicly accessible.

Documented Decisions

Material scientific, architectural, legal, and policy choices should include a written rationale, responsible parties, and reviewable history.

Independent Review

Software, datasets, standards, releases, and scientific records should be open to inspection by qualified third parties.

Responsible Stewardship

Maintainers should preserve attribution, document uncertainty, protect project integrity, and disclose conflicts of interest.

Role Separation

Scientific review, software maintenance, infrastructure operation, funding, and public policy should not be conflated into one unchecked authority.

Continuity Planning

Archives, documentation, credentials, release procedures, and operational knowledge should survive turnover in individual maintainers or institutions.
