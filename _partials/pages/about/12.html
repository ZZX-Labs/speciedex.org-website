Preservation and Resilience

Preservation includes more than retaining final datasets. It also includes schemas, source provenance, transformation logic, provider snapshots, validation reports, licenses, documentation, software, checksums, signatures, and the information required to interpret old records correctly.

Public releases should be reproducible, independently downloadable, and suitable for storage by universities, museums, libraries, governments, research groups, community operators, and individual archivists.

Biodiversity records may outlive the institutions, software platforms, funding programs, and web services that originally created them. Speciedex therefore treats preservation as a primary design requirement rather than a secondary archival task.

Portable Archives

Snapshots should use documented formats that can be copied, inspected, restored, migrated, and verified without proprietary services.

Multiple Mirrors

Independent mirrors reduce reliance on one host, provider, network, jurisdiction, organization, or administrative account.

Integrity Metadata

Checksums, manifests, signatures, release identifiers, and timestamps help detect corruption, substitution, or unauthorized modification.

Backward Compatibility

Migration tools, versioned schemas, historical mappings, and compatibility notes help preserve access as formats and software evolve.

Reconstruction

Canonical sources, provider snapshots, build logic, and documentation should allow public products to be regenerated after loss or infrastructure failure.

Institutional Independence

No single institution should be the only location where the public archive, release history, or operating knowledge can be recovered.
