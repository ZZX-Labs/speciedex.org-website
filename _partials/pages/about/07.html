Data and Scientific Standards

Canonical records should never conceal their inputs. The system should expose which provider supplied a value, how it was transformed, which conflicts remain unresolved, and what level of scientific or editorial review has occurred.

A species record may include accepted names, synonyms, authorities, taxonomic hierarchy, provider identifiers, common names, distribution, habitat, ecology, conservation status, media, genetic references, specimens, publications, relationships, revision history, and explicit uncertainty.

Speciedex is designed to preserve source attribution and distinguish reviewed scientific information from provisional submissions, historical names, disputed classifications, incomplete records, community observations, and automated inferences.

Stable Identifiers

Internal identifiers should survive taxonomic revisions, synonymization, record merges, name changes, and provider migration.

Source Attribution

Imported and contributed data should retain source, license, publication, author, institution, retrieval date, and transformation history.

Revision History

Changes should identify what changed, when, why, by whom or by which process, and how the prior state can be recovered.

Validation States

Records may be verified, reviewed, provisional, disputed, historical, deprecated, incomplete, restricted, or awaiting expert review.

Open Formats

Exports should use documented machine-readable formats that can be archived, inspected, migrated, converted, and reused without proprietary software.

Interoperability

Established biodiversity standards and external identifiers should be supported where technical compatibility, licensing, and scientific meaning permit.

Explicit Uncertainty

Conflicting classifications, uncertain identifications, incomplete ranges, and provisional relationships should be represented rather than hidden.

Reproducible Processing

Normalization, reconciliation, validation, and export procedures should be documented and repeatable from the same inputs.
