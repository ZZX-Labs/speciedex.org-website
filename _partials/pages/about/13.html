Privacy and Sensitive Data

Restrictions should themselves be documented. A record may remain publicly discoverable while exact coordinates, personal information, specimen-security details, or protected source material are withheld or represented at a safer level of precision.

Speciedex should not expose sensitive information merely because it is technically obtainable. Publication decisions should consider conservation risk, legal restrictions, indigenous data sovereignty, research ethics, contributor privacy, collection security, and the possibility of exploitation.

Open biodiversity data can create unintended risks. Exact locations of endangered species, nesting sites, breeding populations, rare specimens, protected habitats, private collections, and culturally sensitive ecological knowledge may require restriction, delay, or generalization.

Location Generalization

Sensitive coordinates may be reduced in precision, delayed, restricted, or replaced by broader regional descriptions.

Contributor Privacy

Personal information should be collected only when necessary and handled under published privacy and data-use policies.

Restricted Records

Public metadata may be retained while details that could endanger species, habitats, communities, collections, or research sites are withheld.

Responsible Disclosure

Security, privacy, data-quality, and conservation-risk concerns should have documented reporting, review, and remediation procedures.

Indigenous Data Responsibility

Traditional knowledge and culturally governed ecological information should respect community authority, consent, access rules, and attribution.

Auditability

Redaction, generalization, and access decisions should be documented so users understand why information is unavailable or imprecise.
