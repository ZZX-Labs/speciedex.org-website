About the Speciedex Project

A complete explanation of why Speciedex exists, how it is organized, which principles govern it, and how it is intended to serve science and the public over the long term.

The About page defines the project itself. It does not repeat the global site header, the root-page ecosystem overview, the landing page’s visitor routing, or the home dashboard’s operational status. Instead, it documents the purpose, boundaries, design philosophy, scientific responsibilities, governance expectations, preservation strategy, and public obligations of Speciedex.

Speciedex is an open biodiversity infrastructure project built to reconcile, preserve, connect, and distribute knowledge about life on Earth. It brings together taxonomy, provider provenance, scientific literature, conservation information, geography, media, genetic references, specimen records, software, archives, and public tools without pretending that any one source or institution is sufficient on its own.

The project is organized as one complete system supported by twelve specialized platforms. Those platforms share identifiers, schemas, validation rules, archival formats, provenance records, release procedures, and synchronization methods while serving different scientific, technical, educational, and operational needs.

The sections below explain the mission and vision of Speciedex, its scientific and technical principles, its thirteen-platform architecture, its Bitcoin-native design, its handling of standards and sensitive data, its governance and development model, its preservation strategy, its long-term roadmap, and the many ways individuals and institutions can participate.
