Who Speciedex Serves

The same underlying records can support very different forms of work. Interfaces, exports, permissions, and explanations should therefore be designed for both specialist and public use without changing the provenance of the underlying data.

Researchers

Search, compare, cite, validate, export, analyze, and reproduce structured biodiversity information across providers and revisions.

Taxonomists

Review authorities, synonyms, type material, classifications, specimens, provider disagreements, publications, and historical change.

Conservation Organizations

Access distribution, habitat, protection, conservation status, source evidence, and carefully generalized sensitive-location information.

Museums and Collections

Link specimens, collections, catalog identifiers, publications, media, and institutional records to stable taxonomic references.

Educators and Students

Explore biodiversity through accessible records, maps, media, explanations, citations, timelines, and learning tools.

Developers

Build applications using open datasets, documented APIs, reusable libraries, schemas, terminal tools, and verifiable releases.

Governments and Institutions

Publish, preserve, reconcile, mirror, and integrate biodiversity information within public services and long-term archives.

The Public

Discover species, understand biological relationships, inspect sources, download open information, and participate in public science.
