Mission

Speciedex exists to organize, preserve, verify, and freely distribute biodiversity knowledge through open standards, open software, transparent scientific provenance, reproducible data processing, and long-term public archives.

Biological information is currently distributed across journals, museums, universities, governments, field guides, research programs, conservation organizations, genetic repositories, mapping systems, databases, and private collections. Each may contain authoritative or valuable material, yet the wider body of knowledge remains fragmented by incompatible identifiers, formats, interfaces, licenses, update practices, and institutional boundaries.

Speciedex seeks to provide the common infrastructure needed to connect those resources without erasing their distinctions. Imported records should retain their provider identity, source identifiers, licenses, retrieval history, transformations, uncertainty, and relationship to the canonical record.

The mission is therefore larger than building another searchable database. It includes preserving evidence, exposing disagreements, supporting reproducible exports, maintaining stable references, enabling independent verification, and ensuring that public biodiversity knowledge can survive changes in software, institutions, funding, and technology.
