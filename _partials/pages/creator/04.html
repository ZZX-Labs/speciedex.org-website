Core Principles

The following principles guide the creator’s work on architecture, data integration, documentation, public interfaces, releases, preservation, and collaboration.

Scientific Accuracy

Information should be traceable to providers, publications, authorities, specimens, genetic repositories, conservation assessments, and reviewable evidence.

Open Knowledge

Documentation, schemas, software, public references, and educational resources should remain accessible and reusable wherever licensing and ethical responsibilities permit.

Engineering Excellence

Software should favor maintainable architecture, explicit interfaces, validation, tests, migration paths, observability, and long-term operational stability.

Provider Provenance

Imported values should retain their original source, identifier, license, retrieval date, transformation history, and relationship to the canonical record.

Independent Verification

Releases, datasets, manifests, archives, and software should be independently verifiable through source references, checksums, signatures, tests, and reproducible procedures.

Long-Term Preservation

Data, software, documentation, media, and historical classifications should remain portable, reconstructable, and recoverable across institutions and technologies.

Responsible Publication

Sensitive biodiversity data, personal information, cultural knowledge, licenses, and conservation risks require careful publication rather than indiscriminate openness.

Community Participation

Researchers, educators, developers, institutions, archivists, operators, and volunteers should be able to contribute through documented and reviewable processes.
