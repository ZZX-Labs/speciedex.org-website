Connectivity

Peer Discovery and Transport

Peer Discovery

Nodes discover peers through configured seeds, peer exchange, signed directories, local-network discovery, institutional lists, and manually trusted endpoints.

Multiple Transports

The protocol can operate across ordinary Internet links, Tor services, private institutional networks, mesh links, removable media, delayed transfer, and other documented transports.

Encrypted Sessions

Transport encryption protects sessions in transit while content hashes, signatures, manifests, and chain ancestry independently verify the data itself.

Resumable Exchange

Inventory comparison, bounded requests, chunked object transfer, and checkpoints allow interrupted synchronization to continue without restarting a complete dataset.

Capability Negotiation

Peers advertise supported protocol versions, schemas, object classes, compression methods, transport features, subscriptions, and service roles.

Trust Separation

A peer may be trusted for connectivity or availability without being trusted to define valid scientific or chain state.

Rate and Resource Controls

Nodes can enforce connection limits, request budgets, object-size limits, timeouts, concurrency controls, and abuse protections.

Private and Institutional Peering

Institutions may maintain authenticated peer groups or private transport paths while continuing to verify all synchronized public data.
